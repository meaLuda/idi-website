Reimagining Tradition: How Men and Boys Are Driving Change in Migori County

In Migori’s Kuria community, a new approach is challenging harmful traditions by engaging men and boys as allies for women’s rights.

Case Study
Project Summary

A New Question

Among the Kuria people of Migori County, Kenya, female genital mutilation (FGM) is a deeply rooted tradition, practiced by 84% of the community. It marks a girl’s transition into womanhood and often leads to early marriage.

For generations, efforts to protect girls focused mainly on women. We asked a different question: Could men and boys — the cultural gatekeepers — be part of the solution?

Listening Before Leading

"Our journey began with introspection. We trained our team in human-centered design, grounding our work in empathy and collaboration to co-create solutions directly with the community."

Listening to the Community

Through three weeks of immersive field research, we spoke with 40 community members across generations, listening to their stories rather than just collecting statistics.

Listening to Stories

Engaging in early conversations with religious leaders, elders, young women, and men revealed that many had never questioned the consequences of practices like FGM.

Shifting Perspectives

One young man shared, "I never thought FGM was harmful; it was just what we did. Now I see how it affects my sisters." These insights guided the solutions we co-created.

Actionable Insights

By understanding the deep-rooted cultural context, our team converted qualitative feedback into creative design sprints for maximum community ownership.

Turning Insights into Action

Community-Driven Ideas

During a design sprint, we co-designed and launched simple, direct interventions to reshape cultural roles.

Men-Led Dialogues

Safe spaces where men and boys openly question and rethink traditional gender expectations.

School Mentorship

Classroom mentorship programs structured to guide young boys on gender equality and rights.

Media Campaigns

Developing media content promoting positive masculinity and rejecting FGM.

Youth Leadership

Empowering young women and men to actively step into leadership roles in the community.

Early Signs of Change

Transforming Tradition

Today, small but powerful shifts are happening across Migori County:

  • Changing Perspectives: More men and boys are questioning harmful practices.
  • Youth Empowerment: Young women and men are stepping into leadership roles.
  • Stronger Collaboration: Communities are building solutions together.
  • Sustained Support: New funding is helping us expand and deepen our impact.

This project is about more than ending FGM. It's about transforming tradition — and creating a future where girls thrive, and men are part of the change.
